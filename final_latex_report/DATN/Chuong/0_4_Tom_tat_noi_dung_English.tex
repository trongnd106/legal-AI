\Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.8cm}

This thesis develops a \textbf{citation-grounded labor law advisory agent and labor contract risk analysis system} based on Microsoft \gls{GraphRAG}~\cite{graphrag2024}, addressing the limitations of traditional keyword-based search~\cite{robertson1994bm25} (inability to perform cross-document reasoning) and general-purpose Large Language Model --- LLM chatbots (prone to hallucination~\cite{ji2023hallucination} and incorrect or outdated citations).

The main contribution is the design of a two-layer ontology with canonicalization: Layer~1 (deterministic) represents the deterministic legal structure without using an LLM, ensuring 100\% consistency for Article/Clause citations; Layer~2 (LLM extraction) represents domain semantics with flexibility. From this design, the system extends to multi-hop reasoning (traversing legal relationships rather than vector similarity), filtering of expired citations, and contract compliance analysis combining deterministic rules with graph grounding.

Results show that the proposed method outperforms pure \gls{LLM} and traditional vector-based \gls{RAG} on questions requiring accurate citations and cross-provision reasoning. The system demonstrate that deterministic legal structure combined with a two-layer ontology is a necessary foundation for reliable legal question answering.

Limitations are a narrow corpus (labor law), and a small evaluation set not yet reviewed by legal experts. However, the design pattern can be extended to other legal domains and reused in similar Legal AI applications. The system is intended for support purposes only and does not replace official legal consultation.
